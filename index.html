What changed, and why

  • Every run now closes with real fillers. 5 cm blanking at each internal corner so the corner door clears the neighbouring drawer handles, plus scribes at the door reveals. v1 summed to exactly 310 on the right wall — it would not have assembled.
  • Dishwasher and sink swapped. The sink now has 60 cm of landing on the left and 50 cm on the right. v1 put the sink hard against the door opening with zero landing.
  • Hob moved up, prep moved down. Hob sits 65 cm off the return wall (min. 40), and PREP 40 + DRAWERS 80 merge into 120 cm of unbroken worktop. v1's longest clear run was 80 cm.
  • Duct shortened ~45 cm to roughly 0.9 m with one bend — box it into a bulkhead under the 2.8 m ceiling.
  • Sink→hob drops from 2.36 m to 1.32 m. Triangle total 6.26 m; every leg inside the 1.2–2.7 m guideline. v1 was 7.05 m.
  • Table is now a 35 cm bar, 110 cm wide. Honest about what it is, and it widens the door-to-door route. 45 cm was too shallow to dine at anyway.
  • Fridge gets a 5 cm spacer and a left hinge so the door clears the architrave and opens past 90° for drawer removal.
  • Four new sockets — a small-appliance zone beside the sink and two above the prep run. Hob and oven each need their own dedicated circuit.

The one question that changes everything

Can the 80 cm opening be closed? That frees 80 cm of the water wall, removes the cross-traffic through the work triangle entirely, and lets the fridge move up to the top-left — which in turn frees the whole bottom wall for a real dining table. It is the single highest-value move available in this room. Everything above assumes it stays.

Verify on site before ordering

  • Wall squareness — measure both diagonals; a U-shape with two corners is unforgiving.
  • Worktop is 62–63 cm deep on a 60 cm carcass; opposite runs each gain 2–3 cm.
  • Does the 100 cm opening have a swinging door? It would foul the fridge.
  • No window is drawn anywhere — confirm whether one exists, and where.
  • Radiator, gas riser, soil stack and existing pipe positions.
  • Consumer-unit spare ways for the hob (32 A) and oven circuits.
  • Hood extraction rate vs. duct length; keep bends to one.
  • Ceiling run to the outlet — confirm the bulkhead depth is acceptable.
